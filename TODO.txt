Excellent question! After analyzing your TOC, I can see you have a solid foundation, but there are some critical gaps that would strengthen the learning progression, especially for complete beginners. Here are my recommendations:

  Important Gaps (Tier 2-4)


  5. basics-mini-batch-gradient-descent.ipynb (Tier 3, before optimizers)

  Why: Optimizers notebook assumes understanding of mini-batches, epochs, and iterations, but these aren't taught.
  - Full-batch vs mini-batch vs stochastic GD
  - Epochs, steps, iterations
  - Why batching helps (noise in gradients)
  - Memory and computational trade-offs

  6. basics-data-normalization.ipynb (Tier 3, with data preprocessing)

  Why: Normalization/standardization is mentioned everywhere but never deeply taught as a standalone concept.
  - Min-max scaling vs standardization
  - When to use each
  - Fitting on train, applying to test (avoiding leakage)
  - Why neural networks need normalized inputs

  7. basics-neural-network-fundamentals.ipynb (Tier 4, BEFORE backprop)

  Why: You jump straight to backprop without explaining what a neural network actually is.
  - What is a layer? What is a network?
  - Forward pass computation
  - Why we stack layers
  - Architecture terminology (width, depth, parameters)
  - Universal approximation theorem (intuition)

  8. basics-computational-graphs.ipynb (Tier 4, BEFORE backprop)

  Why: Backprop is much easier to understand with computational graph visualization first.
  - DAG representation of computations
  - Nodes as operations, edges as data flow
  - Why graphs help with differentiation
  - Visual intuition before diving into autograd

  9. basics-pytorch-fundamentals.ipynb (Tier 4 or early Tier 5)

  Why: Students learn tensors but not the PyTorch ecosystem (autograd, nn.Module, etc.)
  - Autograd basics and .backward()
  - nn.Module and nn.Parameter
  - Building custom layers
  - Model training loop structure

  Nice-to-Have Additions

  10. basics-imbalanced-data.ipynb (Tier 3)

  - Class weights, oversampling, undersampling
  - SMOTE and variations
  - Evaluation metrics for imbalanced data

  11. basics-calculus-refresher.ipynb (Tier 1 or pre-tier)

  - Derivatives, chain rule, partial derivatives
  - Gradient as direction of steepest ascent
  - Critical for understanding optimization

  12. basics-debugging-neural-networks.ipynb (Tier 5)

  - Common failure modes (dying ReLU, NaN losses)
  - Sanity checks (overfit small batch)
  - Gradient flow visualization
  - Learning curve interpretation

  13. basics-hyperparameter-tuning.ipynb (Tier 2)

  - Grid search, random search
  - Bayesian optimization (conceptual)
  - Which hyperparameters matter most

  Recommended Priority Order

  Highest Priority (fill critical gaps):
  1. basics-softmax-multiclass.ipynb (Tier 1)
  2. basics-neural-network-fundamentals.ipynb (Tier 4)
  3. basics-computational-graphs.ipynb (Tier 4)
  4. basics-mini-batch-gradient-descent.ipynb (Tier 3)
  5. basics-probability-distributions.ipynb (Tier 1)

  High Priority (strengthen foundations):
  6. basics-train-test-val-split.ipynb (Tier 2)
  7. basics-data-normalization.ipynb (Tier 3)
  8. basics-pytorch-fundamentals.ipynb (Tier 4/5)

  Medium Priority (round out fundamentals):
  9. basics-numpy-fundamentals.ipynb (Tier 1)
  10. basics-debugging-neural-networks.ipynb (Tier 5)

  The biggest gap I see is that you assume neural network knowledge before teaching what a neural network actually is. The backprop notebook is great, but students need "basics-neural-network-fundamentals.ipynb" and "basics-computational-graphs.ipynb" right
  before it to understand what they're learning backprop for.
